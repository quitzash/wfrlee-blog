Riyadh Layover Guide: RUH Transit Tips and Day Rooms

Make your Riyadh layover at King Khalid International Airport easier with RUH transit tips, visa options, and hourly Wfrlee day rooms from just 70 SAR.

Wfrlee Travel Team · February 3, 2026 · 3 min read · Updated March 5, 2026

A Riyadh layover is increasingly common as the Saudi capital grows into a major business and transit hub. King Khalid International Airport (RUH) handles a steady mix of regional business travelers, long-haul connections, and passengers arriving late at night with a flight out the next morning.

The good news is that RUH is compact and efficient. The challenge is the same as anywhere: a long gap between flights with nowhere comfortable to rest. This guide explains how to handle a Riyadh stopover and why an hourly micro-stay beats a full day rate.

Getting around King Khalid International Airport

RUH has five passenger terminals, numbered 1 through 5, with terminals 1 and 5 handling most international traffic and 5 the newest and busiest of them all.

TerminalMain useTransit notes
Terminal 1International departuresConnected to the main concourse
Terminal 2International arrivalsQuick access to ground transport
Terminal 3InternationalSmaller and quieter overnight
Terminal 4DomesticSimple layout, short walks
Terminal 5Flagship internationalNewest, most amenities and lounges

Terminals are connected by shuttle buses and internal walkways. If you are connecting airside, follow the “Transit” signs and clear a security check without entering the country.

Transfer time estimates

  • Between terminals by shuttle: 20–40 minutes.
  • Immigration and baggage claim: 30–75 minutes.
  • Security and re-check for departure: 45–75 minutes.

A connection under three hours is best spent airside. With five hours or more, leaving the airport or booking a room nearby makes far more sense.

Leaving the airport: visa basics

  • Airside transit. Many nationalities can stay in the terminal without a visa while waiting.
  • Tourist eVisa. The simplest option if you want to leave RUH. Apply online before travel for a one-year, multiple-entry visa.
  • Stopover visa. Some travelers flying with Saudia can obtain a free 96-hour stopover visa that allows them to exit and explore.
  • Visa on arrival. Available to some nationalities, but always confirm with your airline first.

Where to sleep on a Riyadh layover

Airport seating is not designed for rest, and lounges rarely have beds. For a Riyadh layover, a hotel day room or micro-stay gives you a private space to sleep, shower, and reset.

Wfrlee makes this simple:

  • 3 hours — shower, change, and a short nap. From 70 SAR.
  • 6 hours — a full sleep cycle plus time to eat.
  • 12 hours — rest, work, and reorganize before a long-haul leg.
  • 24 hours — for overnight connections and delays.

Because you pay only for the hours you use, a micro-stay can cost up to 75% less than an equivalent overnight room.

A practical Riyadh layover plan

  1. Clear arrivals and store any bags you do not need.
  2. Transfer to your micro-stay and check in.
  3. Shower and set two alarms with a 30-minute buffer.
  4. Sleep for the core of your stopover.
  5. Eat and repack, then head back to the terminal.
  6. Clear security and reach your gate early.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sleep at Riyadh airport? Lounges and seating exist, but for real rest a nearby micro-stay room is more comfortable and often cheaper than a full day rate.

How long should a Riyadh layover be to leave the airport? Plan for at least five hours to allow for immigration, transfers, and returning through security.

How much is a Wfrlee room in Riyadh? Hourly micro-stays start at 70 SAR for 3 hours, with longer options available.

The bottom line

A Riyadh layover does not have to mean a sleepless night on an airport chair. With a clear plan and an hourly micro-stay from Wfrlee, you can arrive at your final destination rested and ready.
